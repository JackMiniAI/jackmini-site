## Executive take

- [observed] The current outreach is getting ignored, not engaged.
- [inferred] The main problem is not just "cold email is hard." The message is too soft, too conditional, and too broad.
- [inferred] The landing page also asks the buyer to understand too much before they trust us.
- [contrarian] I do not think the next move is "send more." I think the next move is "tighten the promise, narrow the buyer, and make touch one feel like a business diagnosis instead of a pitch."

## What the audit found

### 1. The outreach keeps hedging

- [observed] Recent first-touch copy says the listing "may not" make things clear, which weakens the point before the buyer even feels it.
- [observed] Examples from the recent dentist batches:
  - "But your Google listing may not make that as clear."
  - "But your Google listing may still look like a basic general dentist office."
- [inferred] This reads like a vague maybe-problem from a stranger, not a sharp finding worth replying to.

### 2. The CTA is soft and split

- [observed] One version asks for `reply yes`.
- [observed] Another version says `If you want, reply and I can send a few more things I'd fix.`
- [observed] Some versions also push the site on touch one.
- [inferred] That creates too many paths: ignore, click, think about it, maybe reply later.
- [consensus] Cold outreach works better when the ask is tiny and singular.

### 3. The current promise is too broad

- [observed] The site sells an 8-factor audit, competitor breakdowns, templates, posts, descriptions, services optimization, photo strategy, done-for-you, and a retainer.
- [inferred] That is too much cognitive load for a cold prospect who has not yet decided this is a real pain.
- [inferred] We are selling the mechanism before the pain.

### 4. The niche is still too mixed at the inbox level

- [observed] Recent dentist batches included `43` first touches across August 5, August 12, August 19, August 19 at 11:07 EDT, and August 24.
- [observed] `15` of those used generic-like inboxes such as `info@`, `office@`, `hello@`, `reception@`, or similar.
- [inferred] That lowers odds even if the copy improves.

### 5. We already knew the right direction and drifted anyway

- [observed] The electronic-only playbook says cold email should not be a one-shot pitch to buy the audit and that touch one should use one clear observation plus one CTA: reply `audit`.
- [observed] The August 13 hard reset says to go back to one concrete observation in line one and avoid sending people to the site as the main CTA.
- [inferred] The current system drifted from its own best rules.

## CEO diagnosis

- [inferred] Local Map Check should not be sold first as a full audit product.
- [inferred] It should be sold first as a very specific lost-calls diagnosis for one niche.
- [contrarian] I would stop acting like the hero product is the PDF. The hero product is clarity: "here is where your Google listing is costing you calls."

## Revised position

### Narrow target

- [recommended] Focus the next push on dentists only.
- [inferred] They are already the deepest sourced niche in the current workspace.
- [inferred] Their value per new patient is high enough that one extra patient can justify the audit.

### Core promise

- [recommended] New promise: `We find the Google listing issues costing your practice calls.`
- [recommended] Support line: `Plain-English findings. Delivered in 24 hours.`

### Offer ladder

- touch 1:
  - one sharp observation
  - one tiny CTA: reply `audit`
- reply or interest:
  - send 2 real fixes in plain English
- paid offer:
  - $299 audit
- upsell after delivery:
  - $799 done-for-you implementation

### What to remove or push lower

- [recommended] Push the retainer down or hide it completely for now.
- [recommended] Keep done-for-you below the fold.
- [recommended] Lead the page with pain, proof, and simple deliverable before feature inventory.
- [inferred] The current page feels like a service menu. It should feel like a fix for a costly blind spot.
